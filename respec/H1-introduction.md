# Objective and context of Deliverable


## Objective and context 

This Deliverable D1.3.1 T4R Joint Technical Framework presents the findings of Activity 1.3 Jointly create a Technical Design Framework of LDTs as decision support tools in territorial planning, together with local stakeholder groups and stakeholders outside T4R (TCoP) which is part of T4R Work Package 1 (Resilient Strategy).”  

Activity 1.3. includes consideration of:  

- The current state of the art in Technical design of Local Digital Twins;  

- Identify state of the art in other European projects and initiatives.  

The state of the art in other European projects and initiatives was also taken into account.  

  
Part of Activity 1.3, but not part of this D1.3.1, is the formulation of indicators of success (progress) in the Framework’s domain, and how these are measured. This is the topic of D1.3.2.  

Activity 1.3 is one of four Activities in T4R that aim to provide an overview of the experience and expertise among project partners on themes that are key for resilient deployment of Local Digital Twins as decision support tools for territorial planning. These four activities are:  

- A1.3 Jointly create a Technical Design Framework of LDTs as decision support tools in territorial planning, together with local stakeholder groups and stakeholders outside T4R (TCoP).  

- A1.4 Jointly create a Governance Framework of LDTs, to embed them in government decision making processes, together with the Local Stakeholder Groups and stakeholders outside T4R (TCoP).  

- A1.5 Jointly create the Ethics, Inclusion, and Democratization Framework for designing and deploying LDTs together with the Local Stakeholder Groups and stakeholders outside T4R (TCoP).  

- A1.6 Jointly create the Train-the-trainer Framework, together with the Local Stakeholder Groups and stakeholders outside T4R (TCoP).  

The four Frameworks aim to guide cities and regions in the process of developing a local digital twin or a new layer/use case in an existing one. By making explicit room for aspects of governance, ethics-inclusion-democratization, and education-training in these four distinct Frameworks, T4R aims to __support uptake of local digital twins as decision-support tools in territorial planning.__  

The four T4R Frameworks together support the drafting of the Pilot Actions in WP2 (Resilient Pilot Actions), and the development of training modules in WP3 (Resilient People).  

Therefore, the target group of all four Framework deliverables is the T4R Consortium.  Upon use of the Frameworks in WP2 and WP3, the four Frameworks will be finetuned, updated, and merged in A1.10, resulting in D1.10 T4R Joint Implementation Strategy (Starter Kit), which can also be used to inform and consult stakeholders outside T4R. Towards the end of the T4R project, D1.10 T4R Starter Kit, will be updated and finetuned in A1.11, resulting in D1.11 T4R final Joint Strategy (Implementation Handbook), which is for ‘the world’.

## Previous work

This report is building from existing work.

The main publications being used are:

1. Foundation, Data, Visualization, Processes - this is an evolution of a pattern that was raised in the [DUET](https://www.digitalurbantwins.com/) project and proved to be effective for both technical use and organisational use. The evolutive part is the explicit addition of the Foundation element (in DUET this was implicit) and that defines the elements that are needed to bring the other 3 in the same reference frame. These include IAM, Catalogus, Metadata, etc. These concept are also desctibed in the [[LDT]] report.

2. The Policy and Digital Twins [[NLDT_PP_BB]] study indicates that the relationship between Policy and Digital Twins is to be found between Policy steps (Plan Do Act Check - [PDCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDCA#:~:text=PDCA%20or%20plan%E2%80%93do%E2%80%93check,or%20the%20control%20circle%2Fcycle.)) and Capabilities (aka Building Block) of the Digital Twin. Each Policy Step requires its own unique set of Capabilities depending on the Use Case. This means that for each policy step a unique Digital Twin is composed of Building Blocks. To be interoperable, every building blocks defines its own set of standards or best practices.
